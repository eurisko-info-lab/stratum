## Feature

Retention becomes a constituted semantic choice rather than an implementation
accident.

$$
F_{10} = F_9 + \text{constituted semantic retention and archives}
$$

## Meta change

[features/retention/retention.meta](../../features/retention/retention.meta)
declares `RetentionConstitution`, `Checkpoint`, `ArchiveManifest`,
`ArchiveAttestation`, `GcPlan`, `RetainedClosure` and `ReconstructionTarget`.

## Retention modes

```text
CurrentStateOnly
TransitionMetadata
ReplayableFoundation
FullSemanticHistory
CheckpointedHistory
```

Each mode names what must remain derivable. For `ReplayableFoundation`:

$$
\Sigma \vdash F \Downarrow S
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathsf{Retain}_{\rho}(\Sigma) = \Sigma'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Sigma' \vdash F \Downarrow S
$$

## Collection

`PlanCollection` splits a world into `retain` and `discard`. An entry is
discardable exactly when it is not required by the constitution **and** it is
derived **and** all of its regeneration inputs remain retained. Derived caches
such as tool sets, indexes and projections are therefore collected while their
schemas survive, and a stricter constitution that names `tools` as required
keeps them.

`ValidatePlan` rejects any plan whose retained set misses required evidence with
`(invalid required-evidence-removed)`.

## Checkpoints and archives

`ReconstructFromCheckpoint` replays later patches on top of a checkpoint state.
`AttestArchive` produces a stable canonical attestation for an archive manifest,
and the F10 attestation is compared across both hosts by
[test-scala/HostParitySuite.scala](../../test-scala/HostParitySuite.scala).
